Our honesty rule

A HiFi screen never claims something the app does not do. When a screen was found showing something untrue, it was fixed or removed, even when the untrue version looked better:

  • an "end-to-end encrypted" badge on chats that were not encrypted was taken down, and Settings now says exactly where chats are kept;
  • made-up P2P merchants and escrow that released itself 2.6 seconds after "Paid" were replaced by seller-only release, a countdown and disputes;
  • Earn rates up to 34% with no risk wording now say "indicative" and explain the risk and redemption terms.

This document keeps the same rule: every feature carries its real label, next to the feature.

14 · Crypto in chat New

Money moves where the conversation is

People talk about money in chats: who owes what, splitting dinner, a birthday gift, the price of Bitcoin. HiFi puts eight crypto tools behind the coin button in any chat. All of them use the same wallet as the rest of the app, so chat never keeps its own idea of your balance.

PracticeWorks today with practice money. Send and request use the same flow as HiFi Pay (page 14), so you review the amount, coin, recipient, route, every fee and the total before you approve.

1 · Send

Send coin to the person you are chatting with, without leaving the chat. It draws on Funding first and tops up from Spot if it has to, and says when it does.

2 · Request

Ask for an amount. The other person sees the same review screen and pays with one tap; the card shows when it is paid.

3 · Gift packet

Fund a packet up front and choose the number of slots. Members of the chat each claim a random share; claims cost you nothing more.

4 · Split bill

Enter the total and how many people, including you. Each person pays their share, and the card shows who has paid.

5 · Price card

Drop a live quote for any coin into the conversation. Typing a $coin such as $BTC also turns it into a tappable chip.

6 · Price alert

"Tell me when BTC goes above 90,000." Set the alert without leaving the chat.

7 · Slash commands

The same tools from the message box: type /send, /request, /gift, /split, /price, /alert or /tip.

8 · Crypto Desk

A bot conversation in your chat list that answers coin prices and market questions from the live feed.

Coins you can use

Quick tips offer USDT, TREND, BTC, ETH, SOL and DOGE. Send and request work with any coin that has a live price.

A command never leaks

When what you type is a command, the raw "/send 10 USDT" is never posted as a message. You get the review screen instead.

14 · Crypto in chat New

How a chat payment works

From tapping the coin button to both people seeing the payment, a chat payment goes through five steps. Each step is there to stop a mistake or a scam.

1
Tap the coin button, choose SendOr type /send. The panel opens over the conversation, already addressed to the person you are chatting with.
2
Pick the coin and the amountYou see what you can spend and its value at the live price. A coin with no live price cannot be sent by value.
3
Read the review, then approveWho receives it, the amount, the coin, the route, every fee and the total, as in HiFi Pay's flow on page 14.
4
The payment is settled onceThe same payment cannot go twice, even if your connection drops and the app retries. One line is added to your History.
5
A card appears for both of youThe payment card shows in the thread with its status, and the receipt is in History.
The rules crypto in chat keeps
  • One wallet. Chat, HiFi Pay, tips and rewards share one balance and one History.
  • Live price required. A value is never guessed from an old or missing price.
  • Nothing smaller than a coin's smallest unit, so every amount is real.
  • A daily ceiling on money coming in from paid requests and split shares ($5,000 a day in practice), so Request can never act as a money printer.
  • Wallet addresses only appear if that person shared one in that chat.
  • No recovery phrase, ever. HiFi never asks for one in chat, and nobody from support ever will.

Crypto in chat details

Tap a person's name at the top of the chat: send, request, their Pay ID, the wallet addresses they shared, and your recent payments with them. In a group: split a bill or send a gift packet.

Safety checks that come from HiFi Pay

The server decides

Balances and statuses come from the HiFi server, never from the browser. A chat card is not proof of payment; the receipt in History is.

Signed requests

Payment requests and QR codes are signed by the server, so an edited or forged request is refused.

Look-alike names

HiFi checks the name and shows who you are paying, and warns when a handle looks like someone else's.

Paying, funding and deciding together

How money comes into a community, how members back projects, and how the community decides what to do with it.

Paid memberships

Practice
  • Free to join, or a paid membership to join, with optional paid tiers
  • Monthly or yearly, a free trial, upgrades, scheduled downgrades
  • Cancel any time and keep access until the period ends; a 7-day refund window
  • A lapsed membership really removes posting rights and tier roles
  • Pay by card, UPI, Apple Pay or Google Pay, or crypto: USDT, BTC, ETH, XRP, USD1 and more; prices in USD, INR, EUR, GBP or AED
  • Members who joined before a community went paid keep their access

Checkout is a demonstration: it never shows a card, UPI or wallet field and charges nothing.

Marketplace with crypto checkout

Practice

Trusted members (level 30 and up, or managers) sell digital resources such as templates, guides and presets, with a licence (personal, commercial or CC BY 4.0), and can tick Accept crypto.

How a crypto checkout stays safeYour own wallet shows the exact amount and the seller's address before anything moves, and you approve it there. HiFi never holds the funds, never asks for a recovery phrase and never signs for you. Check the first and last six characters of the address. Paying outside the checkout breaks the rules.

Governance: decisions anyone can check

Proposals offer For, Against and Abstain. By default a vote runs for 5 days, needs a quorum of 40 votes, and passes with a For majority above 50%.

Verifiable history: every step (proposal opened, vote cast, result) is a SHA-256 digest in a hash chain. Anyone can recompute it, so a changed record shows.

18 · Security, end to end New

Protecting your account

Most accounts are lost through a stolen session, a phishing page or a fake support agent. Each protection below is aimed at one of those.

Sign-in the server checks itself

Real

The app sends the sign-in token it already holds, and the HiFi server checks it the way Firebase documents for any server: the right signature from Google's published keys, the right project and issuer, not expired. Then two things the token alone cannot say: was this session ended by "sign out of every device", and is the email confirmed?

Two-factor for withdrawals

Ready
  • An authenticator app code (TOTP) on every withdrawal
  • The secret is sealed with AES-256-GCM and bound to your account, so a copied secret will not work on another account
  • 5 wrong codes lock it for 15 minutes; each code works once
  • Turning two-factor off needs a valid code
  • Withdrawals stay closed for 24 hours after two-factor is set up or changed

Prove it is you, at the right moments

Real

Showing card details, unfreezing a card, raising a limit or turning a control back on asks for your password or a fresh Google or Apple sign-in. Protective actions ask nothing: freezing a card or reporting it lost is instant, because a thief gains nothing from them and you may be in a hurry.

Devices, sessions and alerts

Real

See your devices and login activity, sign out everywhere, and turn on new login alerts (a sign-in from a device the account has not seen) and security alerts (password changes and new login methods).

Zero-knowledge keys

Real

Recovery phrases and private keys are never sent to HiFi. There is deliberately no "import by recovery phrase" screen: a page asking for one cannot be told apart from a phishing page, and a real wallet never needs one to connect.

Phishing defences

RealPlanned

Framing is blocked, so no site can lay a fake HiFi over the real one. HiFi Pay warns about look-alike handles, and sending warns about look-alike addresses and network mismatches. Planned: a personal anti-phishing code shown in every HiFi email and in the app.

The same payment cannot go twice

Every money request carries a one-time key. If the connection drops and the app tries again, the server recognises the key:

Two books that never touch

Every account and every ledger line belongs to the practice book or the live book, and the ledger refuses any movement between them. Practice money can never become money you can withdraw.

Compliance, closed by default

Ready

Identity checks, sanctions screening, transaction monitoring and Travel Rule data are slots in the server. An empty slot refuses every live action. There is no "skip compliance" switch, and the server never trusts a level or country the app sends.

Why the signer is separate

Ready

The web server can approve a withdrawal and hold the funds, but it cannot sign one. The signer runs on another machine, listens on no port and asks a hardware device for the signature. Someone who breaks into the web server can add a row to a queue, and nothing more.

18 · Security, end to end · End-to-end encryption New

End-to-end encryption for chats and calls

With end-to-end encryption, a message is locked on the sender's phone and only unlocked on the receiver's. HiFi's servers carry it, but cannot read it, and neither can anyone who breaks into them.

Planned  Where this stands todayToday chats are kept only in your browser: they are never sent to HiFi's servers, and they are not end-to-end encrypted. Settings says exactly that. This design switches on as chats move to servers for delivery and multi-device sync, so that no message ever reaches a HiFi server in a readable form.

Built from published, audited protocols, not home-made cryptography

18 · Security, end to end · End-to-end encryption New

Verifying, recovering and paying privately

Encryption is only as good as the keys behind it. These are the parts that let you check who you are talking to, keep your history across phones, and send money inside an encrypted chat.

Safety codes

Compare a 60-digit code, or scan each other's QR code in chat details, to confirm nobody is in the middle. HiFi warns you when a contact's key changes.

Several devices

Each device has its own keys. A new device is linked by scanning a QR code from one you already use, never by a password alone.

Encrypted backups

Backups are locked with a key only you hold, such as a passphrase or passkey. HiFi cannot read or reset it, so a forgotten key means a lost backup.

Disappearing messages

Already a per-chat setting today. With encryption, both devices enforce the timer.

Less metadata

Sealed sender: the server delivers a message without learning who sent it. Finding contacts uses private lookup, and logs are kept to a minimum.

Reporting abuse

You can forward the messages you choose to moderators. Reporting is your choice; HiFi does not scan the content of encrypted chats.

Crypto inside an encrypted chat

The payment card travels inside the encrypted message, so nobody else can see the note or who it was for. The payment itself is still decided by HiFi's ledger, or by HiFi Chain later, and the card carries a signed receipt. A forged "I paid you" card fails the signature check and shows as unverified.

20 · The future: HiFi Chain and the HIFI coin New Planned

HiFi's own blockchain, and a coin that runs the app

Today HiFi's rewards, community tokens, passes and treasuries are records inside the app. The plan is for HiFi to run on its own blockchain, HiFi Chain, with its own coin, HIFI, used to run the app: paying for actions, rewarding creators and contributors, securing the network and governing it.

Why HiFi needs its own chain

Payments small enough to make sense

A tip of a few cents only works if the network fee is a fraction of a cent. A chain built for HiFi keeps fees tiny and confirms in seconds.

Real ownership

Creators and communities hold their tokens, passes and credentials themselves, and can take them outside HiFi.

Anyone can check

Rewards, treasuries and votes become public records. Today's hash chain for governance moves on-chain.

One economy

One coin connects the feed, chat, communities and the wallet, instead of separate points in each.

Tuned for a social app

HiFi sets the fees, speed and features: sponsored fees, social recovery, built-in name and pass registries.

Works with the wider world

Compatible with Ethereum tools and wallets, with bridges to other chains, so HiFi is never a walled garden.

Design principles

  • Nobody needs HIFI to use HiFi. Everyday fees are sponsored (a "paymaster"), so new users never buy a coin just to send a message.
  • Stablecoins stay welcome. Prices can be in dollars or rupees; HIFI is an option at checkout, not a requirement.
  • Rewards come from real activity, paid from a fixed pool that shrinks over time, never from printing new coins.
  • Fees are recycled to creators, communities and the people securing the chain.

Rules and risks

Not an investmentHIFI is a utility coin for using HiFi. HiFi will not promise price rises or returns, and nothing in this document is an offer to sell any token. Practice TREND balances are not a claim on HIFI; how practice history is recognised, if at all, is decided before launch and published.
Law and taxDepending on the country, a token can count as a virtual digital asset, a payment instrument or a security, with different rules for each. In India, for example, gains are taxed at 30% and a 1% TDS applies to transfers. A lawyer in each country confirms the plan; this is not legal or tax advice.
SecurityAudited contracts, a permanent bug bounty, upgrades that wait behind a public time lock, multi-signature keys for the treasury, and keys for self-custody wallets that HiFi never holds.
Technology riskBridges and new contracts are the most attacked parts of any chain. HiFi starts with a short list of audited contracts, caps what bridges can hold at first, and adds features only after each one is reviewed.
